# Retrieval evaluation results

Generated by `python -m eval.run_eval` against `eval/eval_set.json` (15
hand-labeled questions, one or more correct source papers each) over the
12-paper / 20-chunk corpus, top-k = 5. Full per-query output lives in
[`eval/results/eval_results.json`](../eval/results/eval_results.json).

## Summary

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Queries | 15 |
| Mean recall@5 | **1.00** |
| Mean precision@5 | **0.307** |
| Mean latency | 7.0 ms |

## Reading these numbers

- **Recall@5 = 1.0**: for every one of the 15 questions, at least one chunk
  from the correct source paper was in the top-5 fused (dense + BM25) results.
  On this corpus and query set, hybrid retrieval never missed the right
  paper.
- **Precision@5 = 0.307** is naturally capped well below 1.0 here, not a sign
  the retriever is noisy: the corpus has only ~20 chunks across 12 papers
  (~1–2 chunks per paper), and most eval questions have exactly one relevant
  document. With k=5 and often only 1–2 truly relevant chunks to find, the
  ceiling on precision@5 for a single-relevant-doc question is roughly
  0.2–0.4 even with perfect ranking — the remaining slots are unavoidably
  filled by the next-most-related papers (e.g. a DPR question's top-5 also
  surfaces Contriever and HyDE chunks, which is the correct semantic
  neighborhood, just not the labeled answer).
- Per-query results show every question resolves to `recall_at_k = 1.0`;
  precision varies 0.2–0.6 depending on how many chunks from the correct
  paper exist and rank highly.
- Latency is dominated by BM25 + a local MiniLM forward pass on a tiny
  in-memory corpus — not representative of latency at a larger corpus size,
  where the dense-index lookup and reranking would dominate instead.

## Caveat

This measures **retrieval quality only** (did the right document surface),
not generated-answer faithfulness. See "What I'd do next" in the main
[README](../README.md) for the planned LLM-judge faithfulness/relevance
follow-up metric.
